## Baseline, alternatives, and implementation

The existing diagnostic runner already supplied reviewed local/Slurm execution,
source/executable hashes, serial resource budgets, cancellation, per-trial
intents and committed receipts, and refusal to restart a claimed workspace.
Its campaign cases had no declared randomized factorial design or effect model.
Reusing this runner avoids a second submission engine and keeps account,
allocation, cancellation, and launch review behavior intact.

A simpler comparison of two group means is useful for a single balanced factor
but cannot separate interactions or block drift, establish observed design
rank, or preserve a multi-factor recovery schedule. A generic automatic
optimization framework would require more dependencies and much broader
control authority. The delivered design is bounded to one to four compatible
two-level factors, three to ten repetitions, and at most 160 identified trials.
It uses the existing standard-library statistical helpers and no runtime
numerical dependency. Two-pass modified Gram–Schmidt QR is sufficient for the
small bounded matrix; singular/ill-conditioned designs abstain.

`experiment_design.prepare` emits an ordinary diagnostic recipe plus a seeded,
fully identified schedule. Global or within-block randomization, repeated
all-low controls, literal arguments, declared equivalence, and existing resource
limits are explicit. Incompatible full-factorial combinations fail visibly
instead of disappearing from the design. Operations adds read-only
`factorial-design` and `factorial-analysis` through `science_diagnostics` and
the existing governed `scientific-inspection` worker adapter. Diagnostic launch
remains a separate reviewed action.

The application reports its scalar value/unit, output hash, semantic label, and
effective controls. Missing/mismatched controls are reported and excluded from
effect fitting and control-reference hashes. Bitwise changes, tolerance
failures, and semantic changes remain separate. Main effects and pairwise
interactions use ±1 coding; blocked designs add block intercepts. Results expose
rank, conditioning, residual degrees of freedom, residual variance, and
Bonferroni family Student-t intervals with their assumptions. Zero residuals,
unsupported controls, missing/confounded cells, and insufficient observations
never receive invented inferential certainty. Higher-order interactions,
uncontrolled factors, and measurement noise remain unexplained residual causes;
no causal percentages are reported.

The command-line preparation helper publishes new private directories without
overwrite. Complete schemas, runnable Python/MPI examples, and
[the user guide](../../../guides/factorial-experiments.md) cover workstation and
lab-account use, output formatting, review, cancellation, and recovery.

## Independent review and failure recovery

The state/ownership reviewer found that a globally valid case ID could be
misattributed to another local sequence, and that a missing-receipt branch
bypassed the aggregate read budget. Both are repaired. Collection now:

- Charges every recipe, executable metadata, terminal/process record, intent,
  and receipt immediately against a cumulative 8 MiB limit.
- Binds sequence, case ID, and expanded arguments to that workspace's exact
  ordered recipe; rejects duplicate intents across recovery workspaces.
- Reconciles durable final committed declarations even if an entire trial
  directory disappeared. Missing or inconsistent evidence pauses recovery.
- Takes one terminal-receipt presence decision for the snapshot; disappearance
  during collection cannot change its reconciliation rule.
- Preserves application identity separately from the driver-assigned case ID.
  Active/unknown drivers and uncertain intents cannot authorize replay.

The independent re-review confirmed these corrections. New regressions exercise
swapped valid IDs, changed sequence/arguments, duplicate uncommitted intents,
metadata-heavy/no-receipt budget exhaustion, and deleted committed directories.
The actual cancellation test interrupts after two trials, reviews fourteen
never-started cases in a new workspace, and obtains sixteen exact identities
without repeating committed work. The original workspace remains single use.

## Validation and qualification

Focused regression command:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests/test_experiment_design.py \
  tests/test_diagnostic_trials.py tests/test_input_properties.py \
  tests/test_protocol_contracts.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/check_quality.py
```

The first integrated run passed **78 tests in 16.29 s**, including actual
WorkerClient execution and schema validation. The subsequent control-reference regression passed with the final focused
experiment/driver suite: **57 tests in 15.22 s**. The
quality boundary checks passed: 325 modules, 1,542 import edges, two architectural
contracts, Ruff/format, and three mypy boundary files. Root-owned final regression,
packaging, compatibility, and CI evidence must identify the delivered commit;
these focused results are not substitutes for that gate.

`W37-evaluation.json` preserves source hashes, declared inputs, all actual
observations, known-effect results, and held-out synthetic results. The local
Python and three-rank OpenMPI fixtures each completed sixteen reviewed trials.
Both recovered order/method/interaction contrasts **0.5, 0.5, −0.5**. Their
near-zero residual correctly withheld confidence intervals. The MPI fixture
uses gather plus explicitly controlled root reduction; it does not claim to
control `MPI_Reduce` internals.

OpenMPI 5.0.7 runtime/compiler wrappers existed but development headers were
absent. A SHA-256-verified matching Debian development package was extracted
into an unprivileged temporary prefix. Its URL, digest, size, and compiler flags
are in the artifact. No system packages were changed. After the executor reset
removed temporary files, the prefix was reconstructed and qualification rerun
against the corrected collector. Twenty held-out synthetic designs with known
injected effects achieved **19/20 family interval coverage**. This small
synthetic result does not calibrate intervals for real scientific workloads.

Local MPI qualification explicitly used three CPUs, 1 GiB, eight seconds per
trial and a 120-second overall ceiling; the Python fixture used one CPU,
256 MiB, two seconds/trial and 30 seconds overall. Local memory is the existing runner's inherited per-process `RLIMIT_AS`
ceiling, not an aggregate MPI RSS cgroup; the tiny controlled fixture uses
bounded buffers. No Slurm job was submitted and no GPU/BLAS/site deployment
qualification is claimed. Production Slurm campaigns
reuse the existing explicit account/partition/resource review and verified
cancellation path; opening a report never consumes an allocation.

## Performance and remaining limits

`scripts/benchmark_experiment_design.py` compares simple main contrasts and the
complete validated inference on identical 8-, 16-, and 160-observation inputs,
with CPU/wall times, Python allocation peaks, whole-process RSS, and source/input
hashes. The simple baseline deliberately lacks interaction inference,
conditioning checks, block adjustment, equivalence checks and uncertainty;
it is not a fidelity-equivalent replacement. The controlled window ran alone on the shared host at
2026-10-18 00:31:22.200478–00:31:22.542811 UTC, exit 0, with an unchanged
production source digest. Raw measurements and the window record are retained

The pure analysis makes no scheduler calls or recording writes. These timings
measure local computation, not application runtime, scheduler load under an
actual campaign, or cluster deployment performance.

The implementation and fixtures require no unavailable product decision.
Deployment efficacy remains experimental: representative scientific datasets,
site Slurm qualification, and hardware-specific effective controls require
independently authorized follow-up evidence. No automated campaign deployment,
additional scheduler backend, or allocation consumption is inferred from this
prototype.

## Shared-supervisor integration correction

W31's governed MPI probe exposed an inherited limit boundary in the reused
diagnostic supervisor: an outer CPU hard limit of 30 seconds could not be raised
to the usual 30/31 soft/hard grace pair. The supervisor now clamps each requested
soft and hard CPU, virtual-address-space, and file-size ceiling against the
corresponding inherited limit. It never weakens an existing worker limit.

Two actual nested-subprocess regressions retain CPU 5/30 and 30/30, address-space
256/384 MiB, and file-size 4,096-byte limits while the requested trial limits are
larger. The driver/factorial suite passes 59 tests in 15.80s. This preserves the
existing process-tree cancellation behavior and requires no new supervisor or
scheduler authority. Actual Python/MPI evaluation was refreshed after the controlled benchmark
barrier: all 16 Python and 16 three-rank MPI trials completed, and every retained
source digest matches this driver edit. The 20 held-out synthetic designs were
rerun as well.
